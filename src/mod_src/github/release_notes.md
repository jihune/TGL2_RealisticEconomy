Realistic Economy 1.6.0 for This Grand Life 2, game version v1.03.22.

**New in 1.6.0: better jobs for your household, and a fair price that ends with its research.**

- Better jobs. When a better job opens for a member of your household, one line of the monthly summary and a short one at the top of the screen say so. Two kinds: the same job at the place the member works, when the game would offer more than 6.7% over the pay now (it draws its offer a few percent either way), and the next step of the member's own line of jobs that the member qualifies for now, such as a Business Lawyer who can become a District Judge. A line is the jobs of one career file of the game that ask for the experience the member's job gives. The mod looks when the month's openings are made, after a "Find" for a job and once a game day: experience grows while the member works, so a step can come within reach in the month. `jobs=0` under `[features]`.
- Research of the month. A company's fair price, its blue name in the stock list, its place in the two fair-price orders and today's values in its research block show while its research is of this month. A subscription switched off ends them with the month; before, one research showed them for good.

**From 1.5.1: staff looked after at once, a month planned ahead, and hirings near a month end weighed.**

- A look right away. The mod looked at a business only in the game's block every 91 game hours, and passed over the month's first. A save loaded near a month end, or a business just handed over with nobody in it, waited up to 91 hours with its work undone: in one save a new business left its whole month undone and its reputation fell from 24% to 4%. Now it looks in the hour after a load and after a whole hand-over, and at a month's first hour. In the same save the month left 27% undone and the reputation stayed at 19%. `autoHireNow=0` under `[business]`.
- The month planned ahead. The work of a business's other jobs (an electrician's apprentices, its bookkeeper) comes by chance while the base job is worked, so they were hired one look after another. Now that work is counted ahead from the base job's customers' work, in the shares of the game's own table for the business type, and the month's first look hires for the whole month. Work contracts bring, and a type the table does not have, are hired for when they come. `autoHirePlan=0` under `[business]`.
- Hirings near a month end are weighed. The game pays a whole month's wage at the month end for a person hired at any hour of the month (an electrician hired 55 hours before one was paid the month's $4,120.05). When fewer game hours are left than a candidate's month, nobody is hired for any shortage: the mod weighs the hiring against the reputation the hours keep, as it does for the last few hours, at one month's wage when the job needs the person next month too.
- The reputation of a new business has a price. What its customers pay is the larger of last month's payments and what the business type's table makes of the customers its awareness brings: a new business was paid nothing last month, so its reputation was worth $0 to the mod and its last hours were left undone.

**From 1.5.0: a new business handed over whole gets started, and the window that picks a business's type shows its month.**

- A business nobody works in yet. A business handed over whole (Ctrl+Shift-click on an advert icon) with nobody in it, such as a new one, stayed empty until you hired its first people, and its adverts ran while nobody worked, so its reputation fell. Now the mod hires the first person for each job that has work and nobody doing it, the base job first, and then buys the assets that are short. A job a household member works gets no one. The adverts handed over stay off until someone works.
- Inventory. The game does not work a job whose inventory is out. Handing a whole business over now also switches on the game's box that buys inventory; taking the business back switches the box off only if the mod switched it on.
- A business type's month. In the window that picks the type of a new business, the hover text of a type's coin icon has, under the game's start-up estimate, a month's sales and costs once all its customers come, at your city's prices of today. A new business makes less in its first months. The costs count only the wages of the hours its work needs, so a real business pays more. A type that earns from contracts only says so. `typeEstimate=0` under `[business]`.
- A save loaded after your household's first listing no longer keeps the monthly summary's line that handing over opens after a listing: it says that handing over is open now.

**From 1.4.0: a business's reputation and its last few hours of work, and the automation opens later.** Each has its own setting in `RealisticEconomy.ini`.

- The month's bonus for completed work fades with the work left undone. At a month end the game takes 0.2 times the share of the month's work left undone from a business's reputation, and gives 3.6 points only for a month with nothing left: one hour left of a month's two thousand lost the whole bonus, as much as 18% left costs in penalty. Now the 3.6 points go down with that share and are gone at 2%; the penalty stays the game's. The reputation's hover text in the business window lists both, "completed work" and "incomplete work". Your businesses only. `reputationBonusFade=0` under `[balance]` is the game.
- The last few hours. What the mod's hiring leaves without hands - too little for a hire, no candidate left, or work that comes in after the month's last look - is covered the cheapest way that costs less than the reputation it keeps: the game's outsourcing, where the business may outsource the job (three times the job's standard wage an hour), or a candidate (two months' wages and the hiring cost). Otherwise the reputation is lost. The price of reputation comes from what the business's customers and contracts pay. `autoCover=0` under `[business]`.
- Business automation opens after your household's first listing. Handing staff, assets, adverts, contracts or a whole business to the mod needs the game's "Rivals, Influence and Policies" unlock and a company of your own on the stock market. Until then the click says so, and a save that had something handed over gets it back when it is loaded (the monthly summary names the businesses). `automationEarly=1` under `[business]` opens it from the start, as before 1.4.0.

**From 1.3.1**: in a business's "Purchase Assets" window an item's hover text has what ten hours of it cost (price ÷ lifetime + consumption).

**From 1.3.0: things to spend the money of the late game on, and earlier competition.** Each has its own setting in `RealisticEconomy.ini`.

- Money into influence. A bank has two more donations below the game's "Donate To Political Group" ($100,000, about 100 influence points a month for 12 months), at the same points a dollar: "Fund A Think Tank", $1M, about 1,000 points a month for 12 months, and "Fund A Political Party", $10M, about 10,000. Like the game's donation they need the passion Lobbying. Each can be made once a year: the same donation made again within its 12 months only starts the 12 months again. The city votes on its policies every 12 months (the game: 24); the next vote a save has planned comes on its date. `influence=0` under `[features]` switches both off, `bigDonations=0` and `voteEvery12Months=0` under `[influence]` one of them.
- Personal Coaching, at a hospital below Mental Therapy: one activity for each of the 18 preferences, $500,000 times the price level and 20 hours. When it ends, the chosen preference moves one step towards a like (an allergy becomes a hate, a hate a dislike, a dislike a like; a love a like, an addiction a love). From a dislike to a like the price is taken once more. Each person changes one preference a year; a like already, a second one the same year or too little cash for the second price gets the money back. No happiness points are taken. In the game preferences change only on a birthday, for points. `coaching=0` under `[features]`.
- Competition comes earlier. In a business type's market the firms count 1.25 times beside the custom the city holds, so a type gets crowded sooner and its businesses lose more awareness a month. In one save the competition index of three types went from +9, +7, +2 to +20, +15, +10 (and to +32, +23, +18 at 1.5). `competitionWeight=1` under `[balance]` is the game.
- Korean: more than three hundred names and tips of the game that were wrong or read strangely are put right as the game reads its language files: places ("학년 학교", grade year school, is "초등학교", primary school), the city's policies ("Defund Transportation" read "refund of transit fares"), jobs, status effects, the bank's actions, the words for needs and preferences. The files on the disk are not changed. `wording=0` under `[features]` shows the game's texts.
- Spanish, French, Portuguese and Japanese: the words for how much a person likes something are put right. A like read "Como" or "Comme" ("as"), and in Japanese the like and the love stood one step off. `wording=0` under `[features]`.
- The game's own donation once a year. It could be made every month, but made again it only started its 12 months again, so a donation every month threw away eleven twelfths of the money. Now the household can make it once a year, like the two new ones. `donationOncePerYear=0` under `[influence]` gives the game's monthly donation back.

**From 1.2.1**: four degrees cost a year of the pay of the jobs they open; fifteen more texts of the game put right in several languages; the mod's research-fee line no longer breaks inside the amount in Japanese and Chinese.

**From 1.2.0: balance.** The fee of buying and selling shares is 2% (the game: 1%), the fee of a futures contract 10% of its margin (1%), cash earns interest and a savings account pays 0.8 times the central-bank rate (0.5), a renewed lease takes the market's rent, a business you start has an awareness of 25% (0%), Hydroponics Farm earns what the other types do and the degree Communications costs half. Four faults of the game itself were repaired: a mortgage beyond the amount the bank approved, an auto transfer that bought shares without cash, a tie in a board election that ended the game, a construction contract that never asked for its civil engineer.

**A matter of taste, and how to switch it off**: the README has one table of every feature that changes a rule or a number of the game, with the line of `RealisticEconomy.ini` that switches it off. The description inside the zip begins with the same list in more detail.

**Coming from an earlier version**: extract and run the same file. It replaces the mod's files and leaves your settings file as it was (the saves are not copied a second time). The new features work without a line in an older settings file; to keep the game as it was, add `influence=0`, `coaching=0` and `jobs=0` under `[features]`, `competitionWeight=1` and `reputationBonusFade=0` under `[balance]`, and `automationEarly=1`, `autoCover=0`, `typeEstimate=0`, `autoHireNow=0` and `autoHirePlan=0` under `[business]`.

**Install**: close the game, extract `RealisticEconomy-1.6.0.zip` into the game folder (the one that holds `TGL2.exe`) and double-click `RealisticEconomy_install.bat`. It copies your saves to `saves_before_RealisticEconomy_1` first. To remove the mod, run `RealisticEconomy_uninstall.bat`.

What the mod adds and how to use it is in the README of this repository, in the game's eight languages: English, 한국어, Deutsch, Español, Français, Português (Brasil), 日本語, 简体中文.

The full description is inside the zip (`RealisticEconomy_README_en.txt`, `RealisticEconomy_README_ko.txt`). The SHA256 of the zip is in `RealisticEconomy-1.6.0.zip.sha256.txt`.

An antivirus may question `version.dll`: it is the public Ultimate ASI Loader (MIT), the file that loads the mod when the game starts.
